Calorie tracking on night shifts

When your day runs from evening to morning, a calendar-day calorie total stops making sense. A few small changes fix most of it.

The short answer

On night shifts, track by shift rather than by calendar day, keep the structure of your meals as regular as you can, and judge progress by weekly averages. Eating and sleeping patterns are disrupted, so expect noisier numbers and do not read much into one tired day. Shift work and a history of health problems are good reasons to involve your doctor.

Define your day

Most apps close a day at midnight. If you work 8 pm to 6 am, one shift gets cut into two days, and each looks odd: a tiny total on one, a huge one on the other. You have two options. Either think in "shift days" and add the two halves together in your head or on paper, or ignore daily totals and judge by weekly averages. The second is simpler and works in any app.

Check how your chosen app handles entries after midnight before you commit.

Meal timing that survives a night shift

  • Keep a rough meal skeleton. For example a main meal before the shift, one or two small meals or snacks during it, and a light meal when you get home. Having a pattern matters more than the exact times.
  • Avoid a large heavy meal in the small hours. The British Nutrition Foundation notes that eating late at night, especially high-fat, high-calorie meals, may cause indigestion that can disrupt sleep.
  • Pack food. Vending machines and takeaway at 3 am are where unplanned calories come from. Cut-up fruit, yoghurt, nuts or a prepared meal are easier to log, too.
  • Drink water. The NHS suggests 6 to 8 glasses of fluid a day. Thirst and tiredness are easy to mistake for hunger.
  • Do not skip your wake-up meal. The NHS advises against skipping breakfast, whatever time your first meal is.

Sleep affects the numbers

Tired days often come with more snacking and sweet cravings for many people, though responses differ. Rather than fight it with willpower alone, plan for it: keep an easy default snack, go to bed with a consistent routine, and note sleep hours alongside food. Over a few weeks you will see whether short-sleep days are also high-intake days, which is useful information.

If poor sleep persists, talk to your GP or occupational health service. See the NHS sleep and tiredness pages for general help.

How to log with little energy

  1. Use the quickest method: photo, barcode or a saved meal. At 4 am you will not weigh anything.
  2. Log at the point of eating, not later. Memory after a shift is poor.
  3. Review weekly. Look at average intake across shift days and days off separately. Days off often look different.
  4. Treat transition days gently. The day you switch between nights and days will not look normal. Do not judge it.

Common mistakes

  • Setting a very low target to offset "all the snacking". Rest and food are linked; a gentler target is easier to hold. Our calculators have a 1,200 kcal floor, which is a minimum and not a goal.
  • Comparing yourself with day-shift friends.
  • Using intermittent fasting windows built for a day schedule without adjusting them. If you try fasting, ask your doctor first, particularly with medication or diabetes.
  • Counting caffeine drinks as free. Milk, sugar and syrup count.

Frequently asked questions

How do I track calories if my day crosses midnight?

Either add up the two halves of your shift yourself, or ignore daily totals and judge by weekly averages. The second is simpler.

Should I eat during a night shift?

Many people do better with a planned light meal or snacks than with one heavy meal at night, which may cause indigestion that disrupts sleep, according to the British Nutrition Foundation. Individual needs differ, so ask your doctor or dietitian if you have a health condition.

Does night-shift work make weight loss harder?

It can for some people through sleep disruption and irregular meals, but results vary and I cannot give a number. Focus on routines you can repeat.

Is intermittent fasting good for shift workers?

There is no simple answer. Fasting windows built for day schedules may not fit nights, and it is not suitable for everyone. Ask your doctor first.

Does Pofu have a shift work mode?

No. Pofu has no shift-work mode or sleep tracking. It offers photo, barcode and manual logging, widgets and an Apple Watch app.
