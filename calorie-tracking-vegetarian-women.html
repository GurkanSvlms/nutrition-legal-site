Calorie tracking for vegetarian women

A vegetarian diet is easy to log in calories and harder to judge in nutrients. Here is how to cover both without making it a second job.

By Ali Gürkan Sevilmiş, developer of Pofu · November 7, 2026

The short answer

Track calories as you would anyone, but pay a little extra attention to protein variety and to nutrients that need planning on a vegetarian diet. The NHS says vegetarians need to make sure they get enough iron and vitamin B12. A tracker that shows micronutrients can help, and a blood test through your doctor tells you more than any app can.

What is different for vegetarians

Calories behave the same for vegetarians as for anyone else. What differs is the risk of missing particular nutrients. The NHS says that if you do not plan your diet properly you could miss out on essential nutrients, and it names iron and vitamin B12 as ones to watch. It adds that vegetarians are more likely to have lower iron stores than people who eat meat, because iron from plants is not as easy for the body to absorb, and that including some vitamin C with a meal helps absorption. Dairy or dairy alternatives are the NHS's suggested source of calcium.

Menstruating women are often told to watch iron. I am not a clinician and cannot judge your needs; if you feel unusually tired, have heavy periods or are pale or breathless, ask your doctor about testing rather than guessing from an app.

Protein: variety over perfection

Beans, lentils, tofu, eggs, dairy and nuts all contribute. Instead of counting every gram, aim for a protein source at each meal and note it. Many calorie apps show a daily protein total, which makes gaps visible. Ask your doctor or a dietitian how much protein suits you, especially if you are pregnant, breastfeeding or have kidney problems.

A vegetarian diet can be high in calories without being filling: cheese, nuts, oils and pastries add up. Log them, but you do not need to avoid them.

Logging vegetarian food accurately

  • Check "veggie" entries. Meat substitutes and ready meals vary widely between brands. Barcode scanning beats a generic entry.
  • Estimate cooking oil. Roasted vegetables, falafel and stir-fries absorb a lot, and this is where under-logging happens.
  • Separate plant milks. Sweetened and unsweetened versions differ, as does calcium fortification.
  • Save your staples. Lentil soups, bean chilis and your usual sandwich, so you log them in seconds.
  • Photo logging can misjudge beans, grains and dals because they look alike. Check and correct the estimate.

A simple weekly nutrient check

  1. Pick a calorie range and keep it steady for a few weeks.
  2. Aim for a protein source at every meal and check the weekly average.
  3. Include iron sources the NHS lists, such as beans (kidney beans, chickpeas), nuts, dried fruit and fortified breakfast cereals, with a source of vitamin C at the same meal.
  4. Check your B12 sources: the NHS lists milk, cheese, eggs and some fortified breakfast cereals. Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether you need more than that.
  5. Review the lot after a month and talk to your doctor if something looks off.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a vegetarian diet is automatically low in calories or automatically healthy.
  • Leaving protein to chance and then snacking all evening.
  • Ignoring B12 and iron until symptoms show up.
  • Cutting calories hard on top of a restrictive diet. The more restricted the diet, the more careful you need to be. Our calculators have a 1,200 kcal floor, which is a minimum, not a goal.

Frequently asked questions

Do vegetarians need to track micronutrients?

You do not have to, but the NHS says vegetarians need to make sure they get enough iron and vitamin B12. A tracker with micronutrients can help, and your doctor can test for deficiencies.

How do I get enough iron on a vegetarian diet?

The NHS notes iron from plants is not as easily absorbed, and that including vitamin C with a meal helps. Ask your doctor before taking supplements.

Are vegetarian calories different?

No. Calories are calories. The difference is in making sure you also get enough protein and certain vitamins and minerals.

Is photo logging reliable for vegetarian meals?

It can mix up dishes that look alike, such as dals, beans and grains. Always check the estimate.

Does Pofu track iron or B12?
