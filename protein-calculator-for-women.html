How this calculator works

  • Maintain: 1.4 g of protein per kg of body weight a day.
  • Lose weight: 1.6 g per kg, because more protein helps keep muscle while you eat less and keeps you full longer.
  • Build muscle: 1.8 g per kg, together with strength training.
  • The result is kept between 75 and 230 g a day, and is split evenly over 3 or 4 meals.

The minimum to avoid deficiency for adults is about 0.8 g per kg. The higher values above are for active women and for weight loss, where studies show a benefit. These are the same rules the Pofu app uses.

Sources

  • Jäger R. et al. International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: protein and exercise. J Int Soc Sports Nutr, 2017 (1.4–2.0 g/kg for active people).
  • Morton R.W. et al. Protein supplementation and resistance training: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Br J Sports Med, 2018 (benefit levels off around 1.6 g/kg).
  • National Academies. Dietary Reference Intakes for energy, carbohydrate, fat and protein, 2005 (0.8 g/kg minimum for adults).

The formulas are taken from the published sources above and match the rules the Pofu app uses. This page was not reviewed by a dietitian; it is checked against these sources and updated when they change. How we prepare our content and sources.

Frequently asked questions

How much protein does a woman need a day?

The minimum for adults is about 0.8 g per kg of body weight. For active women and during weight loss, research supports about 1.2 to 2.0 g per kg. For a 65 kg woman losing weight, this calculator gives 104 g a day.

Is 100 g of protein a day too much for a woman?

For most healthy women it is not. It is within the range studied for active people. If you have kidney disease, ask your doctor first.

How much protein should I eat per meal?

Divide your daily target by your meals. With 104 g a day and 3 meals, that is about 35 g per meal, for example a chicken breast or a bowl of Greek yogurt with eggs.

Does protein help with weight loss?

It helps you keep muscle while eating less and it keeps you full longer. Weight loss still comes from eating fewer calories than you use.

Should I use my current or my goal weight?

Use your current weight. If your weight is very high, using a lower reference weight gives a more realistic number; the cap of 230 g a day keeps the result reasonable.
