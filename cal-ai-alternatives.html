Which apps are good Cal AI alternatives?

The closest Cal AI alternatives are other photo-first calorie counters: Pofu, Lifesum, Yazio, Nutrola and the AI features in MyFitnessPal and Lose It!. Why look for a Cal AI alternative?

People usually compare for one of three reasons: they want to try photo logging before paying (Cal AI's listing says scan results require a subscription), they want something beyond the photo such as fasting, macros or cycle context, or they want an app that handles a missed day differently.

Cal AI's strength is focus: its listing describes a short flow of answering lifestyle questions, photographing a meal and reading the breakdown. If that is all you want, it may be the right app. The alternatives differ in what else they add around that flow.

How do I choose between them?

  • You mostly want a quick photo log: Cal AI, Pofu, Lifesum or Nutrola.
  • You want the biggest database or workout logging: MyFitnessPal.
  • You want micronutrient detail: Cronometer.
  • You want cycle context and a gentler tone around missed days: Pofu.

Questions

Is there a free alternative to Cal AI?

Several apps have free tiers. Pofu is free to start: barcode, search and manual logging are free and unlimited, with 3 free AI photo scans on day one and 1 every day after. Cal AI's listing says food scanning analysis results require a subscription (checked November 16, 2026).

What is the best Cal AI alternative for women?

It depends on what you want. Pofu is built for women, with optional cycle awareness, grace days and a gentle fasting timer, on iPhone only. MyFitnessPal and Lose It! are broader trackers with photo features in Premium. See the comparison for women for all six.

Is Pofu a Cal AI alternative on Android?

No. Pofu is for iPhone and iPad only, with an Apple Watch app. There is no Android version right now.

Are photo calorie counters accurate?

They give estimates, not measurements. Portion size is the hardest part, so check the portion and edit what looks off. Pofu shows a confidence score on each estimate. Read our guide to how accurate photo calorie counting is.
