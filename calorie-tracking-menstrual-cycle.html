Calorie tracking and your menstrual cycle

You don't need a different diet for every phase. A few tracking habits make the scale and your appetite much easier to read.

The short answer

Keep one sensible overall target, compare the same cycle days month to month, and judge progress over at least one full cycle. Research suggests that resting energy use may rise slightly in the luteal phase, and that many people eat more then, but both vary a lot between people.

What the research says

  • Resting metabolism: a 2020 meta-analysis of 26 studies found a small increase in the luteal phase, but the effect was smaller and not statistically significant in studies published since 2000. A later systematic review describes an estimated rise of roughly 30 to 120 kcal a day, with inconsistent results between studies.
  • Food intake: a 2025 meta-analysis found people ate about 170 kcal a day more on average in the luteal phase than in the follicular phase, while noting inconsistent study methods.
  • The scale: the NHS lists bloating among common premenstrual symptoms. How much weight this adds varies, and not everyone sees a change. Don't assume every jump is water, and don't assume it's fat either.

These are group averages. They can't tell you what your own body needs in a given week.

How to track across your cycle

  1. Log your cycle start. Without it, you can't tell a pattern from noise.
  2. Compare like with like. Compare day 20 of this cycle with day 20 of the last one, not with last Tuesday.
  3. Use averages. Weekly averages are calmer than daily numbers, and one full cycle says more than one week.
  4. Plan for appetite. If you get hungrier before your period, protein and fiber-rich meals may help, and a small planned increase can be easier than repeated restriction and rebound.
  5. Keep fasting flexible. If you fast and long windows feel harder before your period, a shorter window is a reasonable choice.
  6. Don't cut food because of a high reading. Wait for the trend first.

If your cycles are irregular or have changed, phase-based estimates are less reliable. Trends over several weeks and a conversation with your doctor will help more.

Frequently asked questions

Should I eat more before my period?

Some people feel hungrier then, and a small planned increase may be easier to sustain, but research doesn't support a large surplus. Average differences are modest and vary between people.

Does my calorie target have to change every phase?

No. One sensible overall target with a flexible approach works for many people. Phase-based targets are an option, not a requirement.

Is a jump on the scale before my period always water?

No. Bloating is common, but not everyone gains weight and the amount varies. Compare the same cycle days across months and look at the trend.

Does Pofu predict my period?

No. Pofu's cycle awareness is optional, shows your cycle day and phase, and isn't a period predictor or medical device.
