Cycle calorie calculator for women

How many calories should you eat, and does it change before your period? Get a daily target, a luteal phase target and your protein goal in one step.

How this calculator works

  • Resting energy: the Mifflin-St Jeor equation for women (10 × weight in kg + 6.25 × height in cm − 5 × age − 161).
  • Maintenance: resting energy × an activity factor from 1.2 (mostly sitting) to 1.9 (extremely active).
  • Daily target: maintenance plus your goal (0, −250, −400 or −550 kcal a day), never below 1,200 kcal.
  • Luteal phase target: daily target plus about 5%, kept between 80 and 150 kcal.
  • Protein: 1.6 g per kg when losing weight, 1.4 g per kg otherwise, kept between 75 and 230 g.
  • Cycle phase (optional): days 1–5 count as your period, ovulation as about 14 days before your next expected period, and the days after it as the luteal phase. It never predicts your next period.

These are the same rules the Pofu app uses. They give a starting point; your own trend over a few weeks tells you more.

What the research says

  • A 2020 meta-analysis of 26 studies found a small rise in resting metabolism in the luteal phase, smaller and not statistically significant in studies since 2000. A later systematic review describes roughly 30 to 120 kcal a day, with inconsistent results.
  • A 2025 meta-analysis found people ate about 170 kcal a day more on average in the luteal phase than in the follicular phase.

That is why the allowance is small and optional. These are group averages, not your personal needs.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories should a woman eat a day?

It depends on age, height, weight and activity. Many women maintain their weight somewhere around 1,800 to 2,400 kcal a day; this calculator estimates your own number with the Mifflin-St Jeor equation.

Should I eat more in the luteal phase?

You can allow a little more. Research suggests resting energy use may rise slightly and many people eat about 170 kcal more on average, so this calculator adds a small allowance of 80 to 150 kcal. You don’t have to use it.

Do calories count during your period?

Yes. Any change in energy use across the cycle is small, so calories still count every day.

What is the lowest calorie target this calculator gives?

1,200 kcal a day. Going lower without medical supervision is not recommended.

Does this calculator predict my period?

No. It only uses the date your last period started to show today’s phase. It doesn’t predict periods or ovulation.
