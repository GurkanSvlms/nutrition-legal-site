Does your menstrual cycle affect your calorie needs?

Possibly a little, and it varies a lot between people. Here is what many women notice across the cycle and how to track without panic.

The short answer

Possibly, a little, and it differs a lot from person to person. Many women notice changes in appetite, energy and the scale across the menstrual cycle. Research on how much the body's energy needs change is mixed, and any shift is usually modest. It is not a license to eat far more, and it is not a reason to feel you failed.

This page is general information, not medical advice. If you have irregular cycles, pain, or health concerns, talk to your doctor.

What many women notice, by phase

Menstrual
Energy can be low for some, and cravings or cramps may affect what you want to eat. Others feel normal.
Follicular
After your period, many people report steadier energy and appetite.
Ovulation
Some feel energetic; some notice appetite shifts. Experiences vary widely.
Luteal
The days before your period. Many report stronger appetite or cravings, and water retention that can push the scale up by a kilo or more without any change in body fat.

These are common patterns, not rules. Your own pattern may differ, and it can change from cycle to cycle.

Why the scale jumps before your period

Hormonal shifts can make the body hold more water in the days before a period. That shows up as a higher number on the scale even if you ate on plan. It usually settles after your period starts. Reading that jump as fat gain can lead to cutting food at exactly the time hunger is higher, and then a rebound.

What this means for calorie tracking

  • Look at trends, not days. Compare the same phase from one cycle to the next, or use weekly averages.
  • Do not chase every craving, or fight it. Slightly higher appetite in the luteal phase is common. Protein and fiber-rich meals can help some people feel fuller.
  • Keep fasting flexible. If you fast, a shorter window like 14:10 may feel better than a long fast in the luteal phase.
  • Expect noise. Water, salt, sleep and stress all move the scale too.

If you have a history of disordered eating, or are pregnant or breastfeeding, follow your doctor's or dietitian's advice instead of generic tracking tips.

Questions

Do you burn more calories before your period?

Some research suggests a small rise in energy expenditure in the luteal phase, but findings are mixed and the effect varies from person to person. It is generally modest, so it is not a reason to eat dramatically more.

Why am I hungrier before my period?

Many women report stronger appetite and cravings in the days before a period, which is thought to be linked to hormonal changes. Experiences differ, and you do not need to treat it as a failure.

Why does the scale go up before my period?

Often because of water retention, which can add a kilo or more without any change in body fat. It usually settles after your period begins.

Does Pofu predict my cycle?

No. Pofu does not predict your cycle or give medical advice. Cycle awareness is optional and off by default, and if you turn it on, your cycle data stays on your device.

Should I eat differently in each phase?

There is no single rule. Many people simply watch trends over a week or a cycle and adjust gently. If you have medical concerns, ask your doctor or a dietitian.
