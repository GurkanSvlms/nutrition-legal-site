Questions about calories, your cycle and the scale

Short answers to common questions about period weight, calorie targets and tracking, each with sources. Pofu is our app; some answers mention it and say so.

Your cycle and the scale

  • How much weight do you gain on your period?
    Most people gain about 1 to 5 lb (0.5 to 2.3 kg) around their period, and it is mostly water rather than fat.
  • Is weight gain before my period water or fat?
    For most people it is mostly water. Gaining real body fat takes a large calorie surplus over weeks, so a quick jump of a pound or two just before your period is almost always fluid and bloating.
  • When does period water weight go away?
    For many people it eases within a few days after the period starts and is usually gone by the end of it.
  • Do you burn more calories on your period?
    Probably only a little, if at all. Studies suggest resting energy use may rise slightly in the luteal phase, the days before your period, but the effect is small and inconsistent between studies.
  • Should I eat more in the luteal phase?
    A small planned increase is reasonable, but a large surplus is not supported by the research.
  • Do calories count on your period?
    Yes. The idea that food eaten on your period has zero calories is a myth; calories count the same on every day of your cycle.
  • Why am I so hungry before my period?
    Hormone changes in the luteal phase, the days before your period, are linked with bigger appetite and cravings.
  • Is intermittent fasting OK during the luteal phase?
    It can be, but there is no firm evidence either way, and many women find long fasting windows harder in the days before their period.
  • Should I weigh myself during my period?
    You can, but read the number with your cycle in mind.
  • What is the luteal phase?
    The luteal phase is the second half of the menstrual cycle, from ovulation until your next period starts.

Calories and targets

  • How many calories should a woman eat a day?
    The NHS gives about 2,000 kcal a day as a guide for an average woman, but the real number depends on age, height, weight and activity, and many women need noticeably less or more.
  • How many calories should a woman eat to lose weight?
    Take your maintenance calories and subtract a modest deficit, commonly 250 to 500 kcal a day, which is roughly 0.25 to 0.5 kg a week.
  • What is a safe calorie deficit for women?
    A deficit of about 250 to 500 kcal below maintenance each day, which is roughly 0.25 to 0.5 kg of loss a week, is commonly considered safe for most healthy adults.
  • How much protein does a woman need a day?
    The basic requirement for most adults is about 0.8 g per kg of body weight, but many active women or women losing weight aim for roughly 1.4 to 1.6 g per kg.

Tracking habits

  • What should I do after missing a day of calorie tracking?
    Just log your next meal as usual. Do not try to make up for the missed day by eating less or by logging everything from memory, because one gap changes very little.
  • Is it bad to go over my calorie goal one day?
    No. One day over your goal makes almost no difference to your results, because weight follows your average intake across weeks.
  • How accurate are AI photo calorie counters?
    They are good for quick estimates but not exact.
  • Can I count calories without weighing food?
    Yes. A food scale is more precise, but estimating with barcodes, standard portions or photos is accurate enough for most people to see real progress, as long as you log consistently.
