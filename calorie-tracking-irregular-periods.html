Calorie tracking with irregular periods

Most cycle-based advice assumes a predictable 28-day pattern. If yours is not, you can still track, you just need different rules.

By Ali Gürkan Sevilmiş, developer of Pofu · November 7, 2026

The short answer

With irregular periods, keep one steady calorie target and judge it by weekly averages and multi-week trends rather than by cycle phase. Phase-based adjustments rely on knowing where you are in your cycle, which is hard to know when it is unpredictable. Irregular periods are worth mentioning to a doctor, and very low intake or heavy exercise can itself be a cause.

What counts as irregular

The NHS says the average gap between periods is around 28 days and may be a bit shorter or longer. Periods are described as irregular if the gap is less than 21 days or more than 35 days. They are more likely to be irregular when periods first start and when nearing menopause. The NHS lists other common causes: pregnancy, hormonal contraception, losing or gaining a lot of weight, stress and anxiety, and exercising too much. Conditions such as polyendocrine metabolic ovarian syndrome (PMOS, which used to be called PCOS) or thyroid problems can also be behind it.

The NHS also says irregular periods are not usually a sign of a problem, but to speak to a GP if yours are irregular or your normal pattern changes. Calorie tracking is not a diagnosis tool. It cannot tell you why.

Rules that still work

  • One steady target. Without a reliable cycle, skip phase-based targets. A single sensible number is easier to follow and to evaluate.
  • Weekly averages. Compare weeks, not days. For weight, use the average of several weigh-ins.
  • Record period start dates anyway. The NHS suggests using an app or diary to see whether periods are irregular. These dates are useful for your doctor.
  • Give changes at least three or four weeks before drawing a conclusion. If your cycle length varies from 25 to 45 days, even a month can contain very different phases.
  • Do not blame every scale jump on your period. Without a clear cycle, a rise may be water, salt, digestion or fat. Look at the trend.

Eating enough matters

Because the NHS names big weight changes and over-exercising as possible causes of irregular periods, an aggressive deficit is not a neutral choice for this audience. If your cycle changed after you started cutting calories or training harder, that is useful information for your doctor. Our calculators use the Mifflin-St Jeor equation and a 1,200 kcal floor, but a floor is not a recommendation to sit at it. If your periods have stopped or become rare while you are dieting, pause the deficit and see a doctor.

Common mistakes

  • Using a cycle-phase app's targets as if the phase estimate were accurate.
  • Assuming irregular means PCOS or "hormonal" and treating it yourself. Only a doctor can diagnose.
  • Comparing "day 20 to day 20" when your cycles are different lengths.
  • Cutting calories to fix a weight change that may be driven by something else, such as a thyroid issue.
  • Skipping logging after a high-appetite stretch. Appetite swings are information, not failure.

When to see a doctor

The NHS lists these reasons to see a GP: your periods are irregular, last longer than 7 days, you have bleeding between periods, you have irregular periods with weight gain, tiredness, facial hair growth or skin changes, or you are struggling to get pregnant. Also seek advice if you could be pregnant. Bring your period dates and a weekly intake and weight summary. That is a more useful conversation than "I think my cycle is off".

Frequently asked questions

Should I use a different calorie target in each cycle phase if my cycle is irregular?

Probably not. Phase-based targets need a reliable cycle. A steady target judged over weeks is simpler and more honest.

Can calorie counting cause irregular periods?

The NHS lists losing or gaining a lot of weight and exercising too much as common causes of irregular periods. A very aggressive deficit can contribute, so see a doctor if your cycle changes while dieting.

Does Pofu predict my period?

No. Pofu does not predict periods or ovulation. Its cycle awareness is optional, off by default and shows cycle day and phase from the dates you enter.

Is water weight the cause of my scale jumps?

Sometimes. But with an unpredictable cycle you cannot tie a jump to a phase. Compare weekly averages and look at the multi-week trend.

When should I see a doctor about irregular periods?

The NHS suggests seeing a GP if periods are irregular, last longer than 7 days, occur with bleeding between periods or other symptoms, or if you are struggling to get pregnant.
